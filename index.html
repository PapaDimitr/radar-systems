Week 1 · The radar idea

1. What a radar measures and how

A radar transmits an electromagnetic wave and listens for the echo. Range comes from the echo's delay t₀, speed comes from its frequency shift fD (weeks 6–7), and angle comes from where the antenna points (week 8).

R₀ = c·t₀ / 2
c = 3·10⁸ m/s
monostatic = one antenna for Tx and Rx

Three waveform families

CW transmits all the time: it measures Doppler but can't time an echo. Pulsed sends bursts of width τ every PRI, so range comes from timing. FMCW sweeps its frequency, so range comes from a frequency difference (stretch processing, week 5).

Week 2 · DSP for radar

2. I/Q, the data cube and the DFT

The receiver mixes with cos and −sin of the carrier and low-pass filters, which gives a complex baseband signal y = yI + j·yQ. Keeping it complex keeps the phase, and Doppler lives in the phase.

PRF = 1 / PRI
DC = τ / PRI
samples per PRI = PRI·fs
fast time l → range bin
slow time m → pulse number

3. Range, resolution, ambiguity and the matched filter

R₀ = c·t₀/2
B ≈ 1/τ
ΔR = cτ/2 = c/(2B)
Rua = (c/2)(PRI − τ)
ΔRbin = c/(2fs)
k₀ = round(2R₀·fs/c)

Echo timing and maximum unambiguous range

Try it: push the target beyond Rua. The echo from pulse 1 arrives after pulse 2 has gone out, so the radar thinks the target is close. That's range ambiguity. If the echo lands on top of a transmitted pulse, the radar is deaf to it because a monostatic antenna can't listen while transmitting.

Matched filter on a noisy echo

week 3b · week 4 implementation

The signal model your code implements: y[n] = √Pr·x[n − k₀] + w[n], with w complex Gaussian. The matched filter is h[n] = x*[−n] and its output is z = y ∗ h. The peak collects all the pulse energy at the target delay.

Week 4 · Pulse compression

4. Barker codes: long-pulse energy with short-pulse resolution

A long pulse has high energy E = A²τ, which helps detection, but poor resolution cτ/2. Peak power A² is limited by the hardware. The fix: keep the long pulse and flip the sign of N chips of length τc = τ/N. The energy is unchanged, the bandwidth becomes 1/τc, and the matched filter compresses the echo into a peak about τc wide.

E = A²τ (unchanged)
B ≈ 1/τc
ΔRc = cτc/2
compression = τ/τc = N
Barker: peak N, sidelobes ≤ 1

Pulse-compression lab

τc = 1 µs · fs = 4 MHz
Try it: with two targets 300 m apart, switch between Barker and Plain. Both waveforms carry the same energy, but only the Barker code separates the targets (ΔRc = 150 m vs 1950 m). Lower the SNR to −10 dB: the received signal is buried, yet the MF peak still stands out thanks to the processing gain 10·log₁₀(samples in pulse).
